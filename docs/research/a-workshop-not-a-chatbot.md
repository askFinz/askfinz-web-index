# A workshop, not a chatbot

We argued for a single blank box, and rejected it. Here is the argument that lost, why it lost, and what the decision costs us.

**Canonical page:** [https://askfinz.com/research/a-workshop-not-a-chatbot](https://askfinz.com/research/a-workshop-not-a-chatbot)

## Key points

### A workshop, not a chatbot

The design that nearly became askFinz was one blank box. One input, one thread, everything behind it.

### The case for the box, made properly

Start with the version that nearly won, because a rejected approach argued weakly is not a rejected approach at all.

### What the blank box actually asks of you

A blank box is a great interface for an incidental question and a poor one for work, and the reason is not aesthetic. It is that the box moves a step of the job onto you without telling you it has.

### Purpose-built is not the same as narrow

There is a conflation worth untangling, because it is the reason "purpose-built" reads as a downgrade to people who have been burned by rigid software.

### The two failure modes on either side of us

There are two ways to get this wrong and we are trying to sit between them.

### What this shape costs, honestly

There is a wrong-door problem and we have not solved it. If you do not know whether your question belongs in research or in knowledge, structure is working against you in exactly the moment you needed help most.

### Why the shape matters for trust, not just speed

When a tool is arranged for a purpose, you develop an accurate mental model of what it will and will not do. That predictability is not the opposite of power.

### What we would do differently

We would have separated the two questions earlier. For a long time we argued about "chat versus structure" as though it were one decision, when it is two: does the interface have shape, and does context travel between shapes.
