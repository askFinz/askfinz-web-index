## Key points

### Owning your tools again

Count the layers your working day runs on. Your judgement, your files, your history and context, the interface, the capability, the price.

### The rental model, stated fairly

There was a period when software came on a disc, installed on your machine, and worked as long as you chose to use it. You paid once, the tool was yours, and Tuesday's update could not change what your workflow depended on because there was no Tuesday's update.

### The three costs nobody itemises

Control. When your workflow runs entirely inside a closed platform, every change that platform makes is a change to your work.

### The two fixes we rejected

Both of the obvious answers here are wrong, and they are wrong in opposite directions.

### What a healthier position actually looks like

Full ownership in the old sense is not available for this category of work. But the spectrum between "fully owned" and "fully rented" is wide, and where a tool sits on it is a design decision, not a fact of nature.
