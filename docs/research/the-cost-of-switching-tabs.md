# The hidden cost of switching tabs

The expensive part of a context switch is not the switch. It is the climb back — and the climb never returns you to where you stopped.

**Canonical page:** [https://askfinz.com/research/the-cost-of-switching-tabs](https://askfinz.com/research/the-cost-of-switching-tabs)

## Key points

### The hidden cost of switching tabs

The expensive part of a context switch is not the switch. The switch takes a second.

### Nobody thinks of themselves as a tab-switcher

They think of themselves as someone getting work done. The tabs are infrastructure: the route between having a question and finding an answer, between noticing something and doing something about it.

### What actually gets lost

The productivity literature on interruption is well established, and tool-switching is a form of interruption even when you are the one initiating it. Each transition requires a small reconstruction: where was I, and what was I looking for?

### Three fixes that do not work

Fewer tools. The intuitive answer, and the one most teams try first.

### What consolidation actually has to mean

The useful version is not fewer tools. It is that the tools know what each other are doing.

### Where this argument is weaker than it sounds

Not every switch is a cost. Some are a break, and breaks are load-bearing.

### What we would do differently

The natural instinct when building connected surfaces is to carry everything across, because carrying more feels like more helpfulness. It is not.
