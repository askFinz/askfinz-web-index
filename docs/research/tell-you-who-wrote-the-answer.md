# We tell you which model wrote your answer

Hiding the model is the industry default and it is a design choice, not a neutral one. It asks you to extend more trust than the situation earns.

**Canonical page:** [https://askfinz.com/research/tell-you-who-wrote-the-answer](https://askfinz.com/research/tell-you-who-wrote-the-answer)

## Key points

### We tell you which model wrote your answer

The industry default is to hide the model. The answer arrives from nowhere in particular — no name on the door, no indication of what produced it or what that thing is bad at.

### Attribution is ordinary, and we removed it

When a colleague answers a question, you factor in what you know about them without noticing you are doing it. Their background, their tendencies, the areas where they are excellent and the areas where they are confidently approximate.

### What we show

When you receive an answer in askFinz you can see which model produced it, the sources it drew on, and where a claim came from retrieved material rather than from the model's own reasoning. On models you can read about what is available and what each is known to be weak at.

### Two alternatives we tried and rejected

Show the model only on request. The compromise version: keep the interface clean, put attribution behind a disclosure.

### Citations are not a footnote

A result that cites is not merely more rigorous. It is differently useful, because it converts a closed conclusion into an open thread.

### What visibility does not fix

A model name is not a quality signal. Knowing which model answered tells you what to be alert to.

### What we would do differently

We would have shown the absence of a source with the same emphasis as the presence of one.
